STRATEGY6 Task 5 audit
======================

Transcript slice reviewed
-------------------------
- Move 18: `...f5`
- Move 20: `...fxe4`
- Move 22: `...cxd4` / the central recapture
- Move 24 onward: development versus kingside pawn lunges
- Move 32 onward: clearer winning routes versus rim retreats / shelter loosening

Findings
--------
1. The forcing central sequence itself was not the main problem: the engine already liked `...fxe4` and the clean `...cxd4` recapture.
2. The practical issue came immediately after that transition. At deeper search, the engine could drift into flashy kingside pawn pushes such as `...g5` / `...h5` even though quieter moves like `...Bf5`, `...Nd6`, and later simplification choices kept the advantage cleaner.
3. Those pawn pushes were being treated too optimistically because existing ordering and king-safety logic did not add enough extra cost for loosening a castled shell during a tactical middlegame transition unless the attack was already very dangerous.
4. The best low-risk fix was to add shared tactical-transition guidance:
   - reward safe captures, especially central recaptures,
   - keep a root/ordering preference for cleaner winning transitions,
   - penalize unforced castled-shelter pawn pushes while queens remain on the board unless the attacking pressure is already genuinely dangerous.

Task 5 implementation direction
-------------------------------
- Added transcript-backed regressions for:
  - the clean `...c6d4` central recapture,
  - preferring `...Bf5` over `...g5`,
  - preferring `...Nb5d6` over `...Na7`,
  - rejecting `...h5` once White has castled and cleaner transition moves exist.
- Added `chess_game/chess/tactical_transition_guidance.py` so quiet ordering, root tie-breaks, and evaluation can share tactical-transition shelter and simplification heuristics instead of scattering another set of one-off rules.
